SAP MM Consignment Procurement: Info Records, 411 K Movements & MRKO Settlement Masterclass

Dive deep into SAP MM consignment procurement. Learn how to configure master data, execute 411 K movements, and master MRKO settlement for vendor-owned stock.

As an SAP MM consultant in India, you'll often encounter business scenarios where your client needs to manage vendor-owned stock on their premises. This is known as consignment procurement, a powerful strategy that allows companies to reduce working capital and improve cash flow by only paying for materials when they are actually consumed.

However, effectively managing this process in SAP MM—from setting up the correct master data to executing stock movements and ultimately settling invoices—requires a precise understanding of transaction codes like ME11, movement types such as 411 K, and the crucial settlement transaction MRKO. Without this knowledge, you risk stock discrepancies, incorrect vendor payments, and operational inefficiencies.

This article will provide you with a practical, first-hand guide to SAP MM consignment procurement. You'll learn the 'why' behind each step, master the essential configurations, understand the stock movements, and become proficient in using MRKO for accurate and automated vendor settlements. By the end, you'll be equipped to implement and troubleshoot consignment processes in any S/4HANA environment.

⚡ Key takeaways
  • Consignment procurement delays payment until material consumption, offering significant working capital benefits.
  • The consignment info record (ME11) is essential for pricing and drives MRKO settlement.
  • Movement type 101 K brings stock into the plant (vendor-owned), while 411 K triggers consumption and financial liability.
  • MRKO automatically settles consumed consignment stock, generating payables based on info record prices.
  • Proper configuration of OBYC (KON) and diligent info record maintenance are crucial to avoid settlement errors.
  • MB54 provides a clear overview of all consignment stock available at your plant.

01Consignment Procurement Fundamentals: The Zero Working Capital Advantage

Consignment procurement in SAP MM refers to a business process where a vendor supplies materials to a customer (your company) and stores them on the customer's premises. The crucial distinction here is that the vendor retains legal ownership of these materials until they are withdrawn from consignment stock and consumed by the customer. Your company only incurs a liability and makes a payment once the material is actually used.

The primary advantage of consignment procurement, especially relevant for Indian manufacturers and logistics firms dealing with high-volume, standard components, is the 'zero working capital' benefit. Since you don't own the stock until consumption, your funds are not tied up in inventory. This significantly improves cash flow and reduces inventory holding costs, making it a win-win for both the vendor (guaranteed shelf space) and the customer (deferred payment).

Many companies in India leverage consignment for frequently used, low-value components or strategic raw materials, reducing their inventory carrying costs and freeing up capital for other investments. This model is particularly beneficial when managing volatile material prices or ensuring continuous supply without immediate financial commitment.

02Master Data Configuration: The Foundation of Consignment

Correct master data setup is non-negotiable for successful consignment processing. Without it, your system won't know the pricing, account determination, or even that a material is consignment-eligible. Here are the key components:

1. Consignment Info Record (ME11/ME12)

The consignment info record is the single most critical master data element for consignment procurement. Unlike standard info records, it dictates the price at which you will settle with the vendor when you consume the material from consignment stock. SAP uses the consignment info record to derive the price for MRKO settlement.

  1. Go to transaction code ME11 (Create Info Record).
  2. Enter Vendor, Material, Purchasing Org, and Plant. Select 'Consignment' as the Info Category.
  3. On the 'General Data' screen, ensure all relevant fields are populated.
  4. Navigate to the 'Purchasing Organization Data 1' screen. Here, you must maintain the 'Net Price' for the consignment material. This is the price per unit your company will pay when the material is consumed.
  5. Maintain condition types if a pricing procedure is associated with the info record.
Always ensure the consignment info record is active and contains a valid price. If the system cannot find a valid consignment info record with a price at the time of consumption, MRKO settlement will fail, leading to reconciliation issues.

2. Material Master (MM01/MM02)

While not specific to consignment, ensure the material master is correctly set up for procurement:

  • Valuation Class: Crucial for account determination.
  • Purchasing Views: Standard purchasing data, order unit, etc.

3. Vendor Master (FK01/FK02 or BP in S/4HANA)

Ensure the vendor master record is complete, especially payment terms, as these will influence MRKO settlement.

4. Account Determination (OBYC - Transaction Key KON)

For consignment, you are not actually making a 'purchase' at the Goods Receipt (101 K). The liability only arises when you consume the material (411 K). SAP uses a specific transaction key, KON (Consignment Liabilities), for this automatic account determination. You need to configure the relevant G/L accounts for this transaction key.

This setup is part of your overall SAP MM OBYC Account Determination. For KON, you'll typically assign a liability account (e.g., Payables for Consignment) and an expense/inventory account, depending on your valuation strategy. The system debits inventory or expense and credits the consignment payable account when 411 K is executed.

03Executing Consignment Stock Movements: From GR to Consumption

Once your master data is in place, the operational flow of consignment procurement involves specific purchase orders and goods movements. This ensures that the system correctly tracks vendor-owned stock and triggers financial postings at the appropriate time.

1. Consignment Purchase Order (ME21N)

The first step is to create a standard Purchase Order (PO) using transaction ME21N. The key difference is that for consignment materials, you must use Item Category 'K' (Consignment) for each line item. This tells SAP that the materials ordered are part of a consignment agreement. Crucially, a consignment PO will not show a net price on the item overview, as the price is determined by the info record and only becomes relevant at consumption.

You can refer to the How to Configure a Complete Purchase Order Cycle in SAP S/4HANA article for general PO creation steps.

2. Goods Receipt into Consignment Stock (MIGO - 101 K)

When the materials arrive from the vendor, you perform a Goods Receipt (GR) using transaction MIGO. For consignment, you use:

  • Movement Type: 101 (Goods Receipt for Purchase Order)
  • Special Stock Indicator: K (Consignment)

Upon posting the 101 K GR, the materials are recorded in your plant's inventory as 'Consignment Stock at Vendor'. The important thing to note is that this movement creates no accounting document for material value because ownership has not yet transferred. The stock is physically present, but financially, it's still the vendor's asset.

3. Consumption from Consignment Stock (MIGO - 411 K)

This is where the actual 'purchase' happens. When you need to use a consignment material, you perform a transfer posting from consignment stock to your own unrestricted-use stock. This is done via MIGO with:

  • Movement Type: 411 (Transfer Posting - Consignment to Own Stock)
  • Special Stock Indicator: K (Consignment)

When you post a 411 K movement, an accounting document is generated. The system looks up the price from the consignment info record (ME11) and uses the KON transaction key to debit your inventory/expense account and credit the consignment payables account. This creates the financial liability towards the vendor.

In an embedded EWM S/4HANA environment, consignment goods receipts and consumption are integrated. While the core MM concepts remain, the physical processing is handled by EWM-specific transactions and documents (e.g., Inbound Delivery, Warehouse Task, Posting Change). However, the underlying MM movement types (101 K, 411 K) are still triggered from the ERP side.

4. Returning Consignment Stock (MIGO - 412 K)

If you need to return consignment stock that was previously consumed back to consignment (e.g., due to excess transfer, before MRKO settlement), you can use movement type 412 K. This reverses the financial posting made by 411 K.

5. Viewing Consignment Stock (MB54)

To get an overview of all consignment stock physically present at your plant but legally owned by various vendors, use transaction code MB54 (Consignment Stock at Vendor). This report is crucial for reconciling physical stock with SAP records and for identifying materials eligible for settlement.

Handling Quality Inspection

Consignment stock can also be received into quality inspection stock (e.g., using movement type 101 K to QI stock). From QI, it can be released to unrestricted use consignment stock, or directly to own unrestricted use via 411 K, depending on configuration and business process. The key is that even in QI, the ownership is still with the vendor.

04MRKO Settlement Masterclass: Automating Periodic Settlements

MRKO is the transaction that brings the consignment process full circle. It's used to automatically settle the liabilities for all consignment materials that have been consumed (i.e., transferred from consignment stock to own stock via 411 K) within a specified period. This transaction generates vendor invoices or credit memos directly in Financial Accounting, eliminating manual invoice processing for consignment materials.

Purpose of MRKO

The main purpose of MRKO is to automate the payment process for consumed consignment stock. Instead of waiting for a vendor invoice, your company proactively settles the amount based on the consumption data recorded in SAP, using the prices from the consignment info records.

Prerequisites for Running MRKO

  1. Consignment Info Record: A valid consignment info record with a price must exist for all consumed materials.
  2. Consumption Posting: Materials must have been consumed via movement type 411 K.
  3. Vendor Master Data: Vendor must be set up for automatic settlement (e.g., via payment program configuration).
  4. Account Determination (KON): OBYC must be correctly configured for transaction key KON.

Running MRKO: Step-by-Step

  1. Go to transaction code MRKO.
  2. Selection Criteria: You'll typically enter the Company Code, Vendor(s), Material(s), and a Posting Date range. The Posting Date range is crucial; it defines the period for which consumption documents (411 K) will be considered for settlement.
  3. Processing Type:
    • Settle: This is the most common option. It creates the actual financial documents (payables) in FI.
    • Display: Shows you which items are due for settlement without creating documents. Useful for reconciliation and checking.
    • Test: Simulates the settlement process, allowing you to identify potential errors without actual postings.
  4. Output List: Choose how you want the results displayed (e.g., per vendor, per material).
  5. Execute the transaction.

If successful, MRKO will generate an invoice document in FI (and potentially a credit memo, if returns have occurred). This document will reference the consignment movements and the prices from the info records, leading to a payable entry for the vendor.

Interviewers frequently ask about the role of MRKO and how it differs from traditional invoice processing. Be prepared to explain the 'pull' versus 'push' mechanism: MRKO is a 'pull' by your company based on consumption, while traditional invoices are 'pushed' by the vendor.

Resolving Price Variances and Settlement Issues

If the price in the consignment info record changes between consumption and settlement, or if the vendor sends a manual invoice with a different price, you might encounter variances. MRKO will typically settle based on the current info record price. For significant discrepancies, manual adjustments or credit/debit memos might be required. Always ensure your consignment info records are kept up-to-date with current pricing agreements.

05Common Challenges and Troubleshooting in Consignment

While SAP MM consignment offers significant benefits, consultants often face issues during implementation or day-to-day operations. Here are some common problems and their solutions:

  • Missing Consignment Info Record: If MRKO fails with a message like 'No price could be determined for material X in plant Y', check transaction ME13 (Display Info Record) for material X and ensure an active consignment info record exists with a valid net price.
  • Incorrect Account Determination (KON): If accounting documents for 411 K or MRKO are missing or incorrect, verify the G/L account assignments for transaction key KON in OBYC for the relevant valuation class.
  • Stock Discrepancies: If MB54 shows different stock from physical counts, investigate recent MIGO postings, potential batch issues, or physical inventory discrepancies.
  • MRKO Not Picking Items: Ensure the posting date range in MRKO covers the dates of your 411 K movements. Also, check that the consumption has actually occurred for the selected vendor/material.
  • Price Changes After Consumption: If the info record price changes between 411 K and MRKO, MRKO will use the price valid at the time of settlement. For historical consumption with old prices, you might need to adjust manually or use specific configuration (e.g., price conditions with validity dates).

As an SAP MM consultant in India, mastering consignment procurement is a definite asset. Interviewers will test your understanding of the process, configuration, and troubleshooting. Here are some questions you can expect:

  1. What is consignment stock, and how does it differ from standard stock in terms of ownership and financial implications?
  2. Which specific master data elements are crucial for consignment procurement, and what is the significance of the consignment info record? (Hint: ME11)

  4. Explain the role of transaction MRKO. When is it executed, and what does it achieve?

?Frequently asked questions

What is the main advantage of SAP MM consignment procurement?

The main advantage is the 'zero working capital' benefit. Your company only pays for the materials when they are consumed, not when they arrive. This reduces inventory holding costs and improves cash flow, as the vendor retains ownership until consumption.

What is the significance of the 'K' in movement types like 101 K and 411 K?

The 'K' stands for 'Consignment'. It's a special stock indicator that tells SAP the material is vendor-owned consignment stock, differentiating it from your company's own stock and triggering specific processes like deferred financial postings and MRKO settlement.

When is the liability for consignment stock created in SAP?

The financial liability for consignment stock is created when the material is consumed, specifically when a transfer posting (movement type 411 K) is made from consignment stock to your company's own unrestricted-use stock. This triggers an accounting document based on the consignment info record price.

Can I return consignment stock to the vendor?

Yes, you can return consignment stock to the vendor. If the stock was never consumed, you can use movement type 102 K to reverse the initial goods receipt. If the stock was already consumed (411 K) and then needed to be put back into consignment before MRKO settlement, you could use 412 K. For returns post-MRKO, specific vendor returns processes might be needed.

What happens if a consignment info record is missing?

If a consignment info record is missing or incomplete (e.g., no price), the system will not be able to determine the price for the consumed consignment material. This will lead to errors during the 411 K movement (in some configurations) or, more commonly, when you try to run the MRKO settlement, preventing the generation of vendor payables.
